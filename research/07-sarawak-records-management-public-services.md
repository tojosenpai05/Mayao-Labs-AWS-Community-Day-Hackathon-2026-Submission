# Records Management Practices in Sarawak State Public Services

**Source:** ResearchGate  
**URL:** https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233990138_Records_Management_Practices_in_Sarawak_State_Public_Services  
**Published:** November 2012  
**Scope:** Sarawak — State Level  
**Type:** Peer-reviewed academic study

---

## Core Argument

Records management practices in Sarawak's state public services were assessed across multiple dimensions — institutionalization, records creation and maintenance, records inventory, scheduling, information access, and transfer/storage. The study reveals that while agencies have formal records structures, **operational practice falls significantly short of standards**, particularly in the area of information access and retrieval.

---

## Methodology

Questionnaire-based study targeting officers across departments and agencies in Sarawak state public services. Assessed against standard records management criteria.

---

## Key Findings

- **Records creation is generally practiced** — agencies do produce and file documents, but consistency and standardization vary widely
- **Records access is the weakest area** — finding and retrieving records is significantly harder than creating and filing them. Access controls, lack of indexing, and physical/digital fragmentation all contribute
- **No consistent records inventory** — most agencies do not maintain an up-to-date inventory of what records exist and where they are stored
- **Transfer and storage practices are inconsistent** — records at different stages of their lifecycle (active, semi-active, archival) are not systematically managed, leading to accumulation of inaccessible legacy records
- **Institutionalization is partial** — records management is policy-mandated but not deeply embedded in day-to-day agency culture, so compliance is uneven

---

## Key Pain Points Highlighted

- **Document retrieval failures** — staff cannot reliably find records even when they know the record exists
- **Poor indexing** — documents are filed but not indexed in a way that makes them searchable
- **Siloed storage** — each department manages its own records with no cross-department discoverability
- **Legacy accumulation** — years of unfiled or poorly-filed records create a backlog that overwhelms any retrieval system
- **No central registry** — Sarawak state agencies lacked a unified records registry, meaning inter-agency document sharing required manual coordination

## Relevance to Problem Statement

This is the most geographically specific source — it directly examines Sarawak state government agencies, the exact context the hackathon problem statement addresses. The retrieval gap documented here in 2012 predates SDEB 2030 and the recent AI push; it represents the legacy baseline that digital transformation efforts must overcome.
