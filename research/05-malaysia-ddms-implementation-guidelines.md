# The Implementation Guidelines of Digital Document Management System for Malaysia Public Sector

**Source:** ResearchGate / Malaysian Academic Research  
**URL:** https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339817485_The_Implementation_Guidelines_of_Digital_Document_Management_System_for_Malaysia_Public_Sector_Expert_Review  
**Published:** February 2020  
**Scope:** Malaysia — National Public Sector  
**Type:** Peer-reviewed academic paper

---

## Core Argument

Malaysia's Digital Document Management System (DDMS) was adopted by the public sector to manage documents and records. Despite measurable benefits, **adoption remains below satisfactory levels**. This paper establishes that the absence of clear implementation guidelines is the primary barrier to wider adoption — agencies are willing but lack a structured roadmap.

---

## Background

Malaysia's e-government initiative launched under Vision 2020 included the Generic Office Environment – Electronic Government Document Management System (GOE-EGDMS) as one of its seven flagship projects. Decades later, adoption is still patchy across agencies, indicating a systemic gap between policy intent and operational reality.

---

## Key Pain Points Highlighted

- **Low adoption rates** — most government agencies still rely on hybrid paper-digital or fully manual document handling despite an existing national DDMS
- **No standardized implementation pathway** — different agencies implemented DDMS in different ways, producing inconsistent outcomes and incompatible systems across departments
- **Gap between policy and practice** — national-level mandates for digital document management have not translated into ground-level operational change
- **Lack of training and capacity** — civil servants are not adequately trained to use DDMS effectively, resulting in continued reliance on familiar manual processes
- **Interoperability failures** — documents created in one department cannot be easily accessed or shared by another due to system incompatibilities
- **Resistance to change** — organizational culture in Malaysian public agencies tends to preserve existing workflows even when better alternatives are available

---

## Key Findings

- Expert review (multiple rounds of Delphi-based expert validation) confirmed that implementation success depends on a structured, phased approach rather than wholesale system rollout
- Agencies that received dedicated training and change management support had significantly higher adoption compared to those that received only the software
- Document search and retrieval was consistently cited as a pain point even among agencies that had deployed DDMS — staff still struggled to find documents efficiently

---

## Relevance to Problem Statement

This paper confirms that Malaysia's government document problem is not simply about the absence of digital systems — **the systems exist but are poorly adopted and ineffective at retrieval**. This is the precise gap the hackathon problem statement targets: transforming document archives into an "intelligent, searchable resource." The DDMS experience also signals that any solution must be simple enough to be adopted by civil servants without heavy retraining.
