# Sarawak Adopts AI to Address Citizen Needs

**Source:** The Edge Malaysia — Digital Edge Weekly, April 13–19, 2026  
**URL:** https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/799399  
**Published:** April 2026  
**Scope:** Sarawak — State Level  
**Type:** Industry/News Analysis

---

## Core Argument

Two years into the Sarawak Digital Economy Blueprint 2030 (SDEB), the state government has begun embedding AI into its public sector. Early results from smaller pilots are positive, but adoption remains uneven — concentrated in state-level digital services and largely absent in the private sector and rural communities.

---

## Key Developments

- **Dayang AI Assistant** launched — the Sarawak government's official AI chatbot for citizen-facing services (welfare inquiries, utility bills, government service navigation)
- **Sarawak Artificial Intelligence Centre (SAIC)** established to coordinate AI policy and adoption across state agencies
- **2026 budget focus** — agriculture and tourism singled out for digital transformation; RM213 million allocated for agriculture modernisation
- Early internal pilots demonstrated improved turnaround times and gave policymakers better visibility into service demand patterns

---

## Key Pain Points and Gaps Identified

- **Adoption concentrated in flagship agencies** — AI tools are concentrated in a few high-profile initiatives (Dayang, SAIC) rather than distributed across all government agencies
- **Private sector lag** — businesses in Sarawak are not adopting AI at a meaningful pace; the government-sector push is not pulling the private sector along
- **Rural-urban divide** — AI and digital services reach urban centers (Kuching, Miri) far ahead of rural communities, creating a two-speed service delivery landscape
- **Ageing population and labour shortages** — Sarawak's fertility rate fell from 2.76 in 2001 to 1.6 in 2023, creating pressure to automate government services to compensate for future workforce shrinkage
- **Internal knowledge management not yet addressed** — the AI focus to date is on citizen-facing services (Dayang), not on internal government document management and knowledge retrieval — exactly the gap the hackathon problem statement targets
- **Data access limitations** — while the SDEB calls for open government data, practical access to machine-readable government knowledge bases remains limited

---

## Relevance to Problem Statement

This article confirms that Sarawak is actively moving toward AI-enabled government services, but the internal document management challenge — how government employees find policies, SOPs, and circulars — has not yet been directly tackled. Dayang helps citizens navigate public-facing services, but there is no equivalent tool for civil servants navigating internal knowledge. This is precisely the gap the hackathon is asking teams to solve.
