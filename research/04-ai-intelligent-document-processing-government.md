# AI-Driven Intelligent Document Processing in Government and Public Administration

**Source:** World Journal of Advanced Engineering Technology and Sciences (WJAETS), 2025  
**URL:** https://wjaets.com/sites/default/files/fulltext_pdf/WJAETS-2025-1059.pdf  
**Scope:** International  
**Type:** Peer-reviewed journal article

---

## Core Argument

Traditional government document processing is slow, labor-intensive, and prone to errors — directly causing inefficiencies and delays in public service delivery. AI-driven intelligent document processing (IDP) offers a path to transform this, but requires careful implementation to address the specific constraints of public administration environments.

---

## Key Pain Points Highlighted

- **Manual processing bottlenecks** — document handling in public agencies relies heavily on human labor for sorting, routing, classifying, and retrieving records
- **Error-prone workflows** — manual data entry and document handling introduce errors that cascade into poor decisions
- **Slow service delivery** — citizens and internal stakeholders experience delays because documents must be located, verified, and interpreted by staff before any action can be taken
- **Volume scale problem** — government agencies produce and receive documents at a volume that human-based systems cannot scale to handle efficiently
- **Unstructured data dominance** — a significant portion of government knowledge exists in unstructured formats (free-text circulars, handwritten notes, scanned documents) that keyword search systems cannot effectively index

---

## AI Solutions Discussed

- **Optical Character Recognition (OCR)** with intelligent document understanding to extract structured data from scanned forms, reports, and circulars
- **Natural Language Processing (NLP)** for document classification, entity extraction, and semantic search
- **Intelligent routing** — AI automatically directs documents to the right department or officer based on content analysis
- **Document summarization** — LLMs compress long policy documents into actionable briefs
- **Knowledge base construction** — transforming static document archives into dynamic, queryable knowledge repositories

---

## Barriers to Adoption in Government

- **Legacy infrastructure** — most agencies lack the technical foundation to run modern AI pipelines
- **Data quality** — historical documents are inconsistently formatted, making ingestion and indexing difficult
- **Change resistance** — staff adoption of AI tools requires significant training and change management investment
- **Security and sovereignty concerns** — public sector data carries sensitivity requirements that complicate use of third-party cloud AI services
- **Budget constraints** — AI infrastructure investment competes with operational priorities in underfunded agencies
