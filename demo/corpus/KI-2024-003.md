Interviewer: Can you explain why the procurement threshold was raised in 2024?

Officer: The reason the threshold was raised is that it had not been reviewed since 2022, and by 2024 it no longer reflected what things actually cost. The RM 20,000 threshold was set when prices were very different. By 2024 a routine order of office furniture for a single district office would exceed it. So we were pushing ordinary low value purchases through a full quotation exercise that took six weeks and cost more in staff time than the saving it produced.

Interviewer: So the rationale was administrative cost rather than the money itself?

Officer: That was the main reason, yes. We analysed eighteen months of transactions before recommending the revision. What we found was that transactions between RM 20,000 and RM 50,000 were about forty percent of all procurement by volume but under eight percent by value. They were consuming most of the administrative effort for very little financial exposure. Raising the threshold to RM 50,000 in 2024 moved that whole band into direct procurement.

Interviewer: Was there concern about weakening controls?

Officer: Yes, and that is why the quarterly register return was kept and why we added the requirement to record a justification for anything above RM 20,000. The control did not disappear, it changed form. Instead of controlling before the purchase through tender, we control after the purchase through the register and audit. That was a deliberate decision and it is the part people forget when they read the circular. The circular tells you the number. It does not tell you why the number was chosen or what we gave up to get it.

Interviewer: Anything else you would want a new officer to know?

Officer: That the threshold is not meant to sit still. It was wrong by 2024 because nobody reviewed it for two years. Somebody should be looking at it again by 2027.
