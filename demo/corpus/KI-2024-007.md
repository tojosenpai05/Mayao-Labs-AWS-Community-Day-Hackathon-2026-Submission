Interviewer: How were records indexed before the current system?

Officer: Each department kept its own register, and each register was organised differently. Some by subject, some by date, some by the name of the officer who opened the file. There was no central index. If you wanted a file from another department you telephoned someone you knew there and asked them to look.

Interviewer: Did that work?

Officer: It worked as long as you knew who to telephone. That is the honest answer. The system ran on personal relationships between about thirty people across the service. When those people retired, the knowledge of where things were retired with them. We lost whole categories of file that way. Not destroyed, just unfindable.

Interviewer: What should a replacement system preserve?

Officer: The reason a file was opened. Our registers recorded the subject but never the reason. Twenty years later you find a file on a boundary dispute and you cannot tell whether it was a routine survey or the beginning of a legal matter, because nobody wrote down why it was opened. If a new system records only what a document is and not why it exists, it will fail the same way ours did.
