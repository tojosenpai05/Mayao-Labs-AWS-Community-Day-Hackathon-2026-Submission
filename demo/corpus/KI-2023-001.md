Interviewer: The circular says fourteen working days notice for annual leave. Where did that come from?

Officer: It came from the harvest season. Many of our officers come from districts where families need them back for a period each year, and the fourteen day rule was set so departments could plan cover for several officers leaving in the same window. It was never about distrust of the officer. People read it as bureaucratic obstruction, but it exists so that district offices do not empty out at the same time.

Interviewer: Is that written down anywhere?

Officer: No. That is exactly the problem. The rule survived, the reason did not. I have watched three rounds of officers apply the rule without knowing what it is for, and because they do not know what it is for they apply it rigidly in situations where it makes no sense.

Interviewer: What about the emergency leave provision?

Officer: The three day retrospective window for emergency leave was added after a case where an officer travelled upriver for a family bereavement and could not get a message out for two days. Before that, there was no provision at all and the absence would have been recorded against him. The three days was chosen because that was how long the journey took.
